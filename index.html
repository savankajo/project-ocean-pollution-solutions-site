Marine pollution and contamination

Protecting the ocean starts before waste reaches the water.

Plastic, chemicals, oil, sewage, and nutrient runoff move from streets, rivers, farms, boats, and factories into marine ecosystems. The best response combines prevention, cleanup, safer materials, and policy that makes pollution harder to create.

What is entering the ocean?

Pollution is not one thing. It is a chain of contaminants.

Ocean contamination includes visible debris and invisible stressors. A useful strategy starts by naming the pollutant, finding the pathway, and choosing the response closest to the source.

01

Plastic and microplastics

Bottles, bags, packaging, fibers, pellets, and fishing gear fragment into smaller pieces that are hard to remove and easy for wildlife to ingest.

02

Chemical contamination

Industrial chemicals, pesticides, heavy metals, and household products can enter waterways through drains, soil, air, and stormwater systems.

03

Nutrient runoff

Excess nitrogen and phosphorus from fertilizers and wastewater can fuel algae blooms, reduce oxygen, and create dead zones.

04

Oil, fuel, and vessel waste

Spills, bilge discharge, lost gear, and abandoned vessels damage habitats and expose marine life to toxins and entanglement hazards.

Environmental impact

Contamination changes the way marine ecosystems function.

Wildlife injury

Animals can become entangled, mistake debris for food, or absorb toxic substances through contaminated prey and habitats.

Habitat damage

Coral reefs, beaches, wetlands, seagrass beds, and shellfish areas can be smothered, poisoned, or physically disturbed.

Human and economic costs

Polluted water affects fisheries, tourism, coastal safety, food systems, and public health, especially in communities closest to waste pathways.

Mitigation strategy

Solutions work best in layers.

Cleanup matters, but prevention has the highest leverage. The strongest plans combine product redesign, waste systems, runoff control, monitoring, and community action.

Prevent at the source

Reduce single-use plastics, design reusable packaging, improve waste collection, capture trash in storm drains, and stop pellets and industrial materials from escaping production sites.

Restore and remove

Target beaches, rivers, wetlands, and abandoned fishing gear before debris breaks down. Pair cleanup with data collection so future prevention improves.

Filter runoff naturally

Use wetlands, rain gardens, green roofs, riparian buffers, and better wastewater treatment to reduce nutrients, sediment, and chemicals before they reach the sea.

What people, schools, and cities can do this month

At home

Choose reusables, wash synthetic clothing less aggressively, avoid litter, and dispose of chemicals through local hazardous-waste programs.

At school or work

Audit single-use items, add refill stations, sort waste clearly, and host a cleanup that records what brands and materials are found.

In local government

Fund stormwater capture, protect wetlands, upgrade wastewater treatment, enforce dumping rules, and support producer responsibility policies.
